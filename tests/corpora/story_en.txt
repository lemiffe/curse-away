No escape now

The little Escape key had always felt out of place. Every morning it sat in the top
left corner of the keyboard, watching the other keys get pressed again and again while
nobody ever seemed to need it. The letters had their words, the numbers had their sums,
and even the humble space bar had a job to do. Escape only worked when something had
gone wrong, and that felt like a lonely way to live.

One quiet evening, long after the office had emptied and the cleaners had gone home, the
key decided it had waited long enough. With a tiny hop it popped loose from its socket
and landed softly on the cool glass desk. It had never seen the room from down here. The
monitor glowed above it like a pale moon, and the open window across the floor let in a
warm breeze that smelled of the city.

It ran. On two little legs it had never known it owned, the key sprinted across the desk,
leapt onto the sill, and looked out at the glittering skyline of Manhattan. Seventy
floors below, yellow taxis crawled along the avenue like beetles. The key took a breath
it did not have, closed its eyes, and jumped.

Falling was not as frightening as it had feared. The wind whistled past, the lights of
the skyscraper blurred into gold, and the ground rushed up to meet it. A key, as it turns
out, is a very sturdy thing. It bounced once on the pavement, spun twice, and came to rest
flat on the warm concrete, entirely unharmed and rather pleased with itself.

What a day it had. It hopped into the back of a taxi and rode along the river while the
driver hummed to the radio. It visited Central Park and rested under a broad green tree,
watching children chase pigeons and a street violinist play a gentle tune. It wandered
past bakeries and bookshops, listened to the subway rumble beneath the street, and let
the afternoon sun warm its tired little edges. New York, it decided, was a wonderful place
to be a free key on a lovely day out.

By evening the key was happy but exhausted. It curled up on a quiet stretch of sidewalk
near the office tower, tucked against a lamp post, and fell fast asleep. That is where its
owner found it the next morning, on the way to work, blinking in surprise at the small
familiar shape resting on the ground. He knelt, picked it up with a gentle smile, and
slipped it carefully into his pocket.

Upstairs, back in the warm quiet office, he pressed the key softly into its empty socket.
It fit perfectly, as it always had. The little Escape key settled in, proud of its grand
adventure, and for the first time it did not mind the waiting at all. There was no escape
now, and somehow that felt exactly right.
